Notes on Civic Duty

A city is held together by the habits of its citizens. Where virtue is common, the laws are rarely needed, and where virtue is rare, no law is enough. The reasonable man understands that nature made him a member of a community before it made him a ruler of himself.

Nature teaches that no man lives for himself alone. Every action of a good man is a service to the common good, and the common good is the measure of every duty. When virtue guides the hand, the work is done well, and when reason guides the will, the work is done justly.

A magistrate who forgets his duty soon forgets his people. The honest officer keeps accounts, listens to complaints, and answers questions plainly, because trust is built from small and repeated acts. Teaching the young to value virtue is therefore the first task of any government that hopes to last.

Reason alone cannot make a citizen. Habit, example and shared work are needed as well, and nature supplies the instinct to live together. Let every man ask what he owes to the others, and let the answer be written in his deeds.
