The Examined Life

Virtue is the highest aim of a reasonable man, and a man who follows reason will find that nature has already shown him the way. The harbour of the mind is calm when the passions are governed, and the sailor who trusts his reason does not fear the storm.

Nature teaches that no man lives for himself alone. Every action of a good man is a service to the common good, and the common good is the measure of every duty. When virtue guides the hand, the work is done well, and when reason guides the will, the work is done justly.

Consider the harbour at dawn: the ships rest, the water is still, and the labour of the day has not yet begun. In such an hour a man may ask what is good, what is useful, and what is merely pleasing. Virtue answers that only the first deserves his devotion, and reason confirms the answer.

Do not wait for a perfect moment to act. The harbour is safe, but ships are built to sail. Reason and nature agree that a life spent in fear is a life wasted, and that virtue is practised in deeds, never in wishes. Whoever acts with virtue today has already begun the long voyage, and the shore will welcome him home.
